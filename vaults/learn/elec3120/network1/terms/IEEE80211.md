# IEEE 802.11 — Wi-Fi

**What it is.** The family of wireless LAN standards, all using **CSMA/CA**, in
an **infrastructure** form (hosts talk via an access point) and an **ad hoc**
form (hosts talk directly).

| Standard | Year | Max rate | Range | Band |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 802.11b | 1999 | 11 Mbps | 30 m | 2.4 GHz |
| 802.11g | 2003 | 54 Mbps | 30 m | 2.4 GHz |
| 802.11n (Wi-Fi 4) | 2009 | 600 Mbps | 70 m | 2.4 / 5 GHz |
| 802.11ac (Wi-Fi 5) | 2013 | 3.47 Gbps | 70 m | 2.4 / 5 GHz |
| 802.11ax (Wi-Fi 6) | 2020 | 14 Gbps | 70 m | 2.4 / 5 GHz |
| 802.11be (Wi-Fi 7) | 2025 | ? | ? | 2.4 / 5 / 6 GHz |

**IEEE** = *Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers*, the standards body.

**Note the shape of that table:** rate grows about 1000×, range about 2×. Faster
modulation needs a better signal, so speed and reach trade off — see
[[terms/modulation]].
